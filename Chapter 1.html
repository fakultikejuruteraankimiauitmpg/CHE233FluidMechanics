1.0What is a Fluid?

A fluid is a substance that deforms continuously under shear stress. Not "deforms a bit and stops" — it keeps going as long as the shear stress is applied.

τ = FA Shear stress = shear force / area

2.0Liquid vs Gas

Both are fluids — but a liquid has a definite volume and can form a free surface, while a gas expands to fill its entire container and never forms a free surface.

Liquid: fixed volume Free surface Gas: fills all space No free surface

3.0Stress & Pressure

Stress is the internal force per unit area within a material. There are two types: normal stress (σ) perpendicular to the surface, and shear stress (τ) parallel to it.

4.0Adhesion, Cohesion & No-Slip

Why does fluid stick to a wall? Two molecular interactions explain it: adhesion (fluid ↔ solid, different substances) and cohesion (fluid ↔ fluid, same substance).

5.0Shear Stress & Viscosity

When neighbouring fluid layers move at different speeds, a velocity gradient exists — and that gradient generates shear stress. This is Newton's Law of Viscosity.

τ = μ · du/dy The heart of viscous flow
