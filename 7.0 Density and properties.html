6C4

Two liquids are mixed together: 3.0 L of density 800 kg/m³ and 5.0 L of density 1200 kg/m³. Assuming the volumes are additive, determine the density of the mixture.

m₁ = ρ₁V₁ = 800 × 3.0 × 10⁻³ = 2.4 kg

m₂ = ρ₂V₂ = 1200 × 5.0 × 10⁻³ = 6.0 kg

Note: the mixture density is a mass-weighted average, not a simple average of 800 and 1200 (which would give 1000).

7C4

A hollow metal sphere has an outer diameter of 10 cm and a mass of 1.2 kg. Determine its average density and analyse whether it will float or sink in water.

r = 0.05 m

V = (4/3)πr³ = (4/3)π(0.05)³ = 5.236 × 10⁻⁴ m³

ρavg = 1.2 / (5.236 × 10⁻⁴) = 2292 kg/m³

ρavg = 2292 kg/m³ → SG ≈ 2.29

Since ρavg > ρwater (1000 kg/m³), the sphere sinks. The hollow core reduces the average density, but not enough to make it float.

8C4

A block of mass 0.9 kg has its volume reduced by 25% while its mass stays the same. Its new density is then 1200 kg/m³. Determine the block's original density and original volume.

Vnew = m / ρnew = 0.9 / 1200 = 7.5 × 10⁻⁴ m³

ρold = 0.9 / (1.0 × 10⁻³) = 900

Voriginal = 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³ , ρoriginal = 900 kg/m³

9C4

A cylindrical container of radius 5 cm and height 20 cm is completely filled with 1.2 kg of a liquid. (a) Find the liquid's density. (b) Half of the liquid is then removed and the container is topped up with water (ρ = 1000 kg/m³). Find the new average density of the contents.

V = πr²h = π(0.05)²(0.20) = 1.571 × 10⁻³ m³

(a) ρ = 1.2 / (1.571 × 10⁻³) = 764 kg/m³

Half liquid remaining: m = 0.6 kg in 7.854 × 10⁻⁴ m³

Water added: m = 1000 × 7.854 × 10⁻⁴ = 0.785 kg

mtotal = 0.6 + 0.785 = 1.385 kg ; V = 1.571 × 10⁻³ m³

ρavg = 1.385 / 1.571 × 10⁻³ = 882

(a) 764 kg/m³   (b) ≈ 882 kg/m³

10C4

An object of mass 4.8 kg has a density of 800 kg/m³. It is then compressed so that its volume decreases by 40%, while no mass is lost. Determine (a) its new volume, (b) its new density, and (c) explain whether the mass changed.

V₁ = m / ρ₁ = 4.8 / 800 = 6.0 × 10⁻³ m³

V₂ = 0.60 V₁ = 3.6 × 10⁻³ m³

ρ₂ = 4.8 / (3.6 × 10⁻³) = 1333 kg/m³

(a) 3.6 × 10⁻³ m³   (b) 1333 kg/m³

(c) The mass is unchanged — compression only reduces the volume, so the same matter is packed into a smaller space, raising the density.

1.8Specific Weight

Specific weight is the weight per unit volume.

ω = ρ g ω = specific weight · ρ = density · g = gravitational acceleration
SI unit
N / m³
Dimension
M L⁻² T⁻²

Easy way to remember

Specific weight = Density × g

Typical value for water:

ω = 9.81 × 10³ N/m³ because 1000 kg/m³ × 9.81 m/s² = 9810 N/m³
Simulation · ω = ρ g Same fluid, different planet → different weight
Specific weight ω
9810 N/m³
Density ρ
1000 kg/m³
Gravity g
9.81 m/s²

Density is a property of the material. Specific weight is a property of the material in a given gravitational field — take the water to the Moon and its density is unchanged, but its specific weight drops by a factor of six.

6C4

An astronaut takes 2.0 m³ of water to the Moon, where g = 1.62 m/s². Determine (a) the mass of the water, (b) its specific weight on the Moon, (c) its specific weight on Earth. Explain why the two specific weights differ.

(a) m = ρV = 1000 × 2.0 = 2000 kg

(b) ωMoon = 1000 × 1.62 = 1620 N/m³

(c) ωEarth = 1000 × 9.81 = 9810 N/m³

m = 2000 kg · ωMoon = 1620 N/m³ · ωEarth = 9810 N/m³

The mass and volume of the water are unchanged, so the density is the same in both places. Specific weight, however, is a force per unit volume (weight = mg), so it scales directly with the local gravitational field.

7C4

A fluid has a specific weight of 7.85 × 10³ N/m³ on Earth. Determine its density, and then calculate its specific weight on Mars where g = 3.71 m/s².

ρ = ω / g = 7850 / 9.81 = 800 kg/m³

ωMars = ρ gMars = 800 × 3.71

ωMars = 2968 N/m³

ρ = 800 kg/m³ · ωMars = 2.97 × 10³ N/m³

8C4

Two fluids each occupy a volume of 0.02 m³. Fluid A has a specific weight of 8000 N/m³; fluid B has a density of 900 kg/m³. Determine which fluid has the greater weight and by how much.

ωB = ρBg = 900 × 9.81 = 8829 N/m³

WB = 8829 × 0.02 = 176.6 N

Difference = 176.6 − 160 = 16.6 N

Fluid B is heavier by ≈ 16.6 N

Comparing densities directly is not enough — you must convert to the same quantity (weight) using ω = ρg before comparing.

(b) WJup = mgJup = 50.97 × 24.79 = 1263.6 N

(c) ω = W / V = 500 / 0.08 = 6250 N/m³

A tank of volume 0.75 m³ contains a fluid of relative density 1.26. Determine (a) the fluid's density, (b) its specific weight on Earth, and (c) the total weight of the fluid in the tank.

(a) ρ = SG × 1000 = 1.26 × 1000 = 1260 kg/m³

(b) ω = ρg = 1260 × 9.81 = 12 360.6 N/m³

(c) W = ωV = 12 360.6 × 0.75 = 9270.5 N

1.9Relative Density / Specific Gravity

Relative density (SG) is the ratio of the density of a substance to a standard density.

For liquids and solids, the standard is the maximum density of water at 4 °C and atmospheric pressure.

SG = ρsubstanceρwater ρwater = 1000 kg/m³ at 4 °C

It has no units and no dimensions — it is a pure ratio of two densities.

Typical values: Water SG = 1.0  ·  Oil SG = 0.9

9C4

A hydrometer floats with 60% of its volume submerged in liquid A and with 75% of its volume submerged in liquid B. Determine the SG of both liquids and the ratio of their densities.

1.10Dynamic Viscosity

Viscosity is the fluid property associated with its resistance to deformation / flow.

Dynamic viscosity can be defined as the shear force per unit area required to move one fluid layer relative to another.

μ = τdu / dy τ = shear stress · du/dy = velocity gradient (shear rate)
SI unit
N s / m²  or  kg / (m s)
Dimension
M L⁻¹ T⁻¹

Typical values

Water  →  1.14 × 10⁻³ kg/(m s)

Air  →  1.78 × 10⁻⁵ kg/(m s)

Important conversion

10 P = 1 kg/(m s)

where P = poise. So 1 P = 0.1 kg/(m s) and 1 cP = 10⁻³ kg/(m s).

Simulation · Falling-Ball Race Same ball, same size — only μ differs (speeds exaggerated)
Thinnest fluid
Water μ = 1.0×10⁻³
Thickest fluid
Glycerin μ = 1.5
Ratio
1500 : 1

The ball in water sinks almost instantly. The same ball in glycerin crawls. That resistance is the fluid's internal friction — its viscosity.

6C4

A flat plate of area 0.5 m² slides over a 1.2 mm thick oil film (μ = 0.9 Pa·s) at a speed of 0.3 m/s. Determine the force required to maintain this motion.

F = τ × A = 225 × 0.5

F = 112.5 N

7C4

Water (μ = 1.0 × 10⁻³ Pa·s) and glycerin (μ = 1.5 Pa·s) are sheared at the same velocity gradient. Determine the ratio of the shear stresses and interpret the result physically.

τ = μ (du/dy), and du/dy is the same for both

τglycerin / τwater = μglycerin / μwater

= 1.5 / (1.0 × 10⁻³) = 1500

τglycerin : τwater = 1500 : 1

8C4

A shaft rotates inside a bearing with a clearance of 0.1 mm, filled with oil of μ = 0.2 Pa·s. If the surface speed of the shaft is 1.5 m/s, determine the shear stress in the oil film.

9C4

A fluid is tested in a viscometer: at du/dy = 100 s⁻¹ the shear stress is 4 Pa, and at du/dy = 300 s⁻¹ the shear stress is 12 Pa. Determine μ and state whether the fluid is Newtonian.

First point: μ = 4 / 100 = 0.04 Pa·s

Second point: μ = 12 / 300 = 0.04 Pa·s

Same μ at both shear rates

1.10Newton's Law of Viscosity

The relationship μ = τ / (du/dy) comes directly from Newton's law of viscosity:

τ = μ dudy shear stress = viscosity × velocity gradient

For a fluid between two parallel plates — the bottom fixed, the top moving at speed U with gap h — the velocity profile is linear, so the shear rate is uniform:

dudy = Uh   ⟹   τ = μ Uh Couette flow — constant shear stress at every height

1.11Kinematic Viscosity

Kinematic viscosity is defined as:

ν = μρ ν = kinematic viscosity · μ = dynamic viscosity · ρ = mass density
SI unit
m² / s
Dimension
L² T⁻¹

Typical values

Water  →  1.14 × 10⁻⁶ m²/s

Air  →  1.46 × 10⁻⁵ m²/s

Why "kinematic"? Dividing μ by ρ removes the mass from the dimensions. What is left — L²T⁻¹ — is purely a motion quantity: it has the same dimensions as a diffusion coefficient. It tells you how fast momentum spreads through the fluid, not how much force it takes.

✓Exam-Friendly Definitions

Mass Density
Density is the amount of matter (mass) contained in a unit volume of a substance. ρ = m / V. Unit: kg/m³.
Specific Weight
Specific weight is the weight of a substance per unit volume. ω = ρ g. Unit: N/m³.
Relative Density / Specific Gravity
Relative density is the ratio of the density of a substance to the density of water at 4 °C and atmospheric pressure. SG = ρ / ρwater. It has no unit and no dimension.
Viscosity (Dynamic)
Viscosity is the fluid property that represents its resistance to deformation or flow. Dynamic viscosity is the shear stress required per unit velocity gradient: μ = τ / (du/dy). Unit: N s/m² = kg/(m s).
Kinematic Viscosity
Kinematic viscosity is the ratio of dynamic viscosity to mass density. ν = μ / ρ. Unit: m²/s.
