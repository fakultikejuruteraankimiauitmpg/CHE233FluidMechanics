Interactive Fluid Mechanics

Liquid
vs Gas

Both are fluids. Both flow under shear stress. But they behave very differently inside a container — and that difference comes down to volume, free surface, and how far apart the molecules are.

Liquid → fixed volume, free surface  ·  Gas → fills all space

1Both Are Fluids

Both liquids and gases are fluids — because both can undergo continuous deformation and flow under shear stress.

But they behave differently when placed in a container. That difference is what this chapter is about.

2Liquid

A liquid has a definite volume but does not have a definite shape.

Simulation · Water in a Glass Adjust the amount, tilt the glass — the surface stays horizontal

Water placed in a glass: the water takes the shape of the lower part of the container, but it does not necessarily fill the entire container.

Why?

  • Liquid molecules are relatively close together, so the liquid tends to maintain approximately the same volume.
  • However, the molecules can move relative to one another. Therefore the liquid flows and takes the shape of its container.

3What is a Free Surface?

A free surface is the boundary between a liquid and the gas above it, when the liquid does not completely fill the container.

Simulation · Free Surface & Gravity Toggle gravity — watch what happens to the surface

In the presence of gravity, the free surface of a liquid at rest is generally horizontal.

A glass containing water has a distinct upper boundary — that boundary is the free surface.

4Gas

A gas does not have a definite shape or a definite volume.

Simulation · Gas Fills the Container Resize the container — the gas expands to fill it

If we put air into a container, the gas spreads throughout the available space. If the container is larger, the gas expands to occupy the additional space.

5Why Does a Gas Fill the Entire Container?

Gas molecules are much farther apart than liquid molecules and can move freely throughout the available space.

Simulation · Molecules Compare liquid (close, slow) vs gas (far apart, fast)

When gas is placed in a container, its molecules spread out until they occupy the available volume.

Gas → expands to fill the entire available space

Everyday example: if you spray perfume in one corner of a room, the smell eventually spreads throughout the room.

6Why Can't a Gas Form a Free Surface?

This is an important distinction.

Simulation · Liquid vs Gas Boundary Left: clear boundary · Right: no boundary

Consider water: there is a clear boundary between the water and the air — the free surface.

Now consider air inside a container: there is no separate horizontal boundary between "gas" and "empty space" inside the container. The gas simply expands and occupies the available volume.

7The Role of Gravity

The phrase "in the presence of gravity" is important.

Simulation · Gravity and the Free Surface Toggle gravity — compare flat vs floating surface

A liquid at rest under gravity forms a free surface that is approximately horizontal.

Gravity pulls the liquid downward, and the liquid settles into a configuration with a horizontal free surface.

This is why water in a glass has a flat top surface.

8The Fundamental Difference

The easiest way to remember the difference is:

Liquid
Definite volume + no definite shape.
It takes the shape of the container but does not necessarily fill it.
Gas
No definite volume + no definite shape.
It expands and fills the entire available space.

9⭐ Exam-Friendly Version

Liquid
  • A liquid has a definite volume but no definite shape.
  • It takes the shape of the container in which it is placed.
  • Under gravity, a liquid at rest can form a free surface.
  • Example: water in a glass.
Gas
  • A gas has neither a definite volume nor a definite shape.
  • It expands until it reaches the container walls.
  • It fills the entire available space.
  • It does not form a free surface.
  • Example: air in a room.
